# Learning Resources for Microfluidics

> Last updated: March 2026

## Textbooks

### Essential Reading

| Book | Authors | Publisher | Level | Best For |
|------|---------|-----------|-------|----------|
| **Theoretical Microfluidics** | Henrik Bruus | Oxford University Press (2008) | Advanced undergrad / graduate | Rigorous mathematical treatment of microfluidic physics. The gold standard for theory. Math-heavy — requires comfort with vector calculus, PDEs, and fluid mechanics. |
| **Fundamentals and Applications of Microfluidics** (3rd ed.) | Nam-Trung Nguyen, Steven Wereley, Seyed Ali Mousavi Shaegh | Artech House (2019) | Intermediate | Comprehensive coverage from fundamentals to applications. Updated with organ-on-chip, droplet synthesis, paper microfluidics. ISBN: 978-1630813642 |
| **Introduction to Microfluidics** | Patrick Tabeling | Oxford University Press (2005) | Beginner / Intermediate | Pedagogical, concept-focused. Cross-disciplinary — great first book. Less math than Bruus, more physical intuition. |
| **Microfluidics for Biological Applications** | Wei-Cheng Tian, Erin Finehout | Springer (2009) | Intermediate | Biology-focused applications, lab-on-chip |
| **Microfluidics for Biologists** | C. Lu, S.S. Bhatt (eds.) | Springer (2016) | Beginner | Ideal for biologists new to microfluidics |

### Specialized Texts

| Book | Authors | Focus |
|------|---------|-------|
| **Microsystem Design** | Stephen Senturia | MIT Press (2001) | MEMS fundamentals — essential for understanding fabrication |
| **Microfluidics and Lab-on-a-Chip** | A. Manz, P. Neužil, J.S. O'Connor, G. Simone | RSC Publishing | Comprehensive with Q&A for students |
| **Electrokinetic and Colloid Transport Phenomena** | Masliyah & Bhattacharjee | Wiley (2006) | Electrokinetics deep dive |
| **Capillarity and Wetting Phenomena** | de Gennes, Brochard-Wyart, Quéré | Springer (2004) | Surface tension, capillary effects |
| **Transport Phenomena** | Bird, Stewart, Lightfoot | Wiley | Classic transport phenomena reference |

### Recommendation

- **Starting from zero?** → Tabeling's "Introduction to Microfluidics"
- **Need the math?** → Bruus "Theoretical Microfluidics"
- **Working engineer?** → Nguyen & Wereley "Fundamentals and Applications"
- **Biologist entering the field?** → "Microfluidics for Biologists" (Springer)

## Getting Started Path

### For a Complete Beginner (Month 1-3)

1. **Read** Tabeling's "Introduction to Microfluidics" (2-3 weeks)
2. **Watch** NPTEL Microfluidics and Nanofluidics lecture series (free)
3. **Explore** Elveflow's online review articles for practical knowledge
4. **Try** 3DuF for simple chip design (free, browser-based)
5. **Build** a simple PDMS chip using soft lithography or order from uFluidix

### For an Engineer Moving into Microfluidics (Month 1-2)

1. **Read** Nguyen & Wereley "Fundamentals and Applications" (focus on chapters relevant to your application)
2. **Simulate** using COMSOL (if available) or OpenFOAM (free) — start with a simple channel flow
3. **Design** using AutoCAD/SolidWorks (you likely already know these) or KLayout for mask layouts
4. **Fabricate** using soft lithography (if you have cleanroom access) or 3D printing (Formlabs Form 3+)

### For a Researcher Going Deep (Month 1-6)

1. **Study** Bruus "Theoretical Microfluidics" — work through the problems
2. **Master** COMSOL Microfluidics Module or FEniCS for custom simulations
3. **Learn** cleanroom fabrication — photolithography, PDMS casting, bonding
4. **Attend** MicroTAS or IEEE MEMS conference
5. **Read** Lab on a Chip journal regularly
6. **Contribute** to Metafluidics or open-source projects
